\section{Purpose}

This item demonstrates that DiCo-NLI data production is operational before the
full shared-task release. The accompanying \texttt{trial/} folder contains a
curated pilot set built from the PhrasIS files supplied with the proposal,
multilingual organizer data, participant-facing track files, hidden gold files,
submission templates, and baseline submissions scored with the official scorer.

The pilot contains 120 source pairs: 30 \texttt{EQUIVALENCE}, 30
\texttt{FORWARD\_ENTAILMENT}, 30 \texttt{BACKWARD\_ENTAILMENT}, and 30
\texttt{NEGATIVE\_OTHER}. For every label, 15 pairs come from PhrasIS image
sources and 15 from headline sources. We deliberately excluded borderline
\texttt{SIMI}/\texttt{REL} examples from this feasibility sample and used clear
\texttt{UNR} examples for \texttt{NEGATIVE\_OTHER}, so the trial data is
inspectable and unambiguous.

\section{Organizer Format}

The internal organizer format is \texttt{trial/trial\_dataset.json}. It is a
grouped JSON file with one object per underlying source pair. Each object stores
the English pair, Spanish and Basque translations, the DiCo-NLI gold label for
\texttt{text1 -> text2}, and provenance metadata:

\begin{verbatim}
{
  "pair_id": "dico_trial_0001",
  "split": "trial",
  "gold_label": "EQUIVALENCE",
  "languages": {
    "en": {"text1": "at 91", "text2": "aged 91"},
    "es": {"text1": "a los 91 años", "text2": "de 91 años"},
    "eu": {"text1": "91 urterekin", "text2": "91 urte zituela"}
  },
  "metadata": {
    "origin": "PhrasIS",
    "source_file": "PhrasIS.train.headlines.positives.txt",
    "source_line": 1,
    "source_modality": "headlines"
  }
}
\end{verbatim}

This grouped format is intentionally not the participant format. It is the
organizer/annotation format: it keeps multilingual variants, source provenance,
and split membership together, which prevents leakage across directions or
languages when the final train, development, trial, and test splits are created.

\section{Participant Files}

Participant files are fixed CSV files generated from the grouped JSON by
\texttt{participant\_dataset\_production}. Participants do not derive directions or mixed
language combinations themselves. The four release tracks are:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{llr}
\toprule
Track & Content & Trial rows \\
\midrule
\texttt{track1} & English monolingual & 210 \\
\texttt{track2} & Spanish monolingual & 210 \\
\texttt{track3} & Basque monolingual & 210 \\
\texttt{track4} & Mixed cross-language only & 1260 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Files derived from the 120 grouped trial pairs.}
\end{table}

For train/trial releases, the participant file includes labels:
\begin{verbatim}
instance_id,pair_id,text1_lang,text2_lang,text1,text2,label
\end{verbatim}

For blind test releases, the participant file omits labels:
\begin{verbatim}
instance_id,pair_id,text1_lang,text2_lang,text1,text2
\end{verbatim}

The hidden gold file consumed by the scorer contains:
\begin{verbatim}
instance_id,pair_id,text1_lang,text2_lang,text1,text2,reverse_pair_id,label
\end{verbatim}

Participant submissions remain minimal:
\begin{verbatim}
instance_id,label
\end{verbatim}

Thus the public interface is simple, while the hidden gold retains the
\texttt{reverse\_pair\_id} links needed for SoftCons and HardCons.

\section{Baseline Feasibility Check}

We simulated two naive participants on every produced track. The random system
samples uniformly from the four labels with seed 2027. The majority system
predicts the most frequent gold label in the produced track; ties are broken by
the official label order, yielding \texttt{EQUIVALENCE} for this balanced trial
set.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{llrrr}
\toprule
System & Track & Weighted F1 & SoftCons & HardCons \\
\midrule
Random & track1 & 0.281 & 0.222 & 0.078 \\
Random & track2 & 0.218 & 0.178 & 0.067 \\
Random & track3 & 0.247 & 0.222 & 0.067 \\
Random & track4 & 0.222 & 0.207 & 0.052 \\
\midrule
Majority & track1 & 0.127 & 1.000 & 0.333 \\
Majority & track2 & 0.127 & 1.000 & 0.333 \\
Majority & track3 & 0.127 & 1.000 & 0.333 \\
Majority & track4 & 0.127 & 1.000 & 0.333 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Naive baseline scores generated with the official evaluator.}
\end{table}

These results align with the metric definitions. Random predictions have low
directional consistency and low F1. The majority system is always internally
consistent because \texttt{EQUIVALENCE} is self-reversing, so SoftCons is 1.0,
but it performs poorly on item-level classification and is hard-correct only for
the equivalence reciprocal pairs. This confirms that the three reported scores
capture different failure modes and that the scorer is operational.
